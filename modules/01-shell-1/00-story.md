# Story: Day one, no mouse

Bob arrives at Caleston on a grey Monday morning with a new badge, a new
laptop and an inbox that already contains forty-one unread messages. Most of
them are automated. One of them is from Priya, the platform team lead:

> Welcome aboard! In three weeks we demo **Project Mercury** to our biggest
> client. You'll be helping us get the servers ready. Your account on the dev
> box is live — log in and have a look around.

Bob opens the remote session and is greeted by a black window with a single
blinking line:

```text
bob@ubuntu:~$
```

No desktop. No icons. No "File → Open". Just a cursor waiting for him to type
something.

Marco, who sits opposite and has been at Caleston for six years, notices the
expression on Bob's face. "First time on a server without a GUI?" he asks.
"Don't worry. Everything on those machines can be done from that prompt —
and once you get used to it, it's faster than clicking. Start by finding out
where you are, then make yourself at home."

Priya has left Bob a small first assignment in the ticket queue: organise the
regional notes folder for the Asia office, clean up a mess left by a previous
contractor, and get his shell configured the way the team likes it.

In this module you will learn, alongside Bob, what a shell actually is, how
to move around the filesystem, how to create and remove files and
directories, where to look for help when you forget an option — and how to
make the Bash shell feel like your own. By the end of the module, Bob's
first ticket will be closed.
